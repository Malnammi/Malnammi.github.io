Moayad Alnammi, Ph.D.

Assistant Professor of Computer Science working on AI and machine learning

I am an Assistant Professor in the Information and Computer Science Department at King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals (KFUPM). I teach artificial intelligence and machine learning at the undergraduate and graduate levels. My research applies machine learning to scientific and engineering problems. Before academia I worked as a system analyst in petroleum engineering software at Saudi Aramco. I hold a Ph.D. and M.S. in Computer Sciences from the University of Wisconsin–Madison and a B.S. in Computer Science from KFUPM (First Honors).

My research sits at the intersection of machine learning and the natural and engineering sciences. My doctoral work at the University of Wisconsin–Madison focused on iterative batched screening and active learning for early-stage drug discovery. The goal was to use models to decide which compounds to test next when experiments are expensive and budgets are finite. Since then my group and collaborators have applied ML in cheminformatics, process planning, manufacturing scheduling, supply-chain risk, and subsurface property prediction. I care about the full path from idea to deployed pipeline. That includes data engineering, distributed computation, model selection, and the unglamorous work of making results reproducible.

A significant part of my recent work concerns agentic AI workflows for research. These are context-engineered development environments in which AI agents draft code and orchestrate experiments. Verification and citation grounding are central safeguards in this work. In scientific work an output you cannot trace is an output you cannot trust. I run my research group on these workflows.
